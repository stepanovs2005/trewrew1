There is an old craft word — verge. It's the name of the smallest, most careful part of a mechanical watch: the piece at the escapement where continuous force becomes a beat, where the movement's tension is released one measured tick at a time. Old watchmakers said the verge had a habit of showing up at the bench before the watchmaker did — that after enough years working the same movements, after enough hands holding the same loupe over the same tiny wheels, the verge stopped being just a component and started being a small presence sitting on the bench beside the open case, watching the hands work. The presence took the name of the part. Verge.
